Super-Soft Burger Buns

Yudane formula, made with a quick microwave scald

Makes 4 large buns (~140 g dough each)Active ~40 minTotal ~5½ hBake 160 °C fan (320 °F convection)

Ingredients

Scald

50 gBread flour16.7%
50 gWater16.7%
150 gMilk50%

Dough

20 gEgg yolk1 large egg; keep the white6.7%
20 gSugar6.7%
6 gSalt2%
250 gBread flour83.3%
4 gInstant yeastor 4.8 g active dry / 12 g fresh1.3%
20 gButter, soft6.7%

Finish

1Egg whitefrom the egg above
Sesame seedsto sprinkle

Totals

300 gTotal flour100%
200 gTotal liquid (water + milk)66.7%
570 gDough weight

Method

  1. Scald. In a tared, microwave-safe mixing bowl, whisk the scald flour, water and milk until smooth. Microwave in 20 s bursts, whisking after each, until it thickens to a glossy pudding at 65 °C (150 °F). This takes about 60–90 s, depending on the milk's starting temperature. At 1:4 flour to liquid it stays gelatinous instead of setting firm.
  2. Cool to ~27 °C (80 °F). Cover and let it rest about 45 min, or set the bowl in ice water and stir for a few minutes if you are in a hurry. Check with the probe; this temperature sets the dough temperature (see note). Make-ahead: cover and refrigerate for up to 1 day, then warm in short bursts back to ~27 °C.
  3. Mix & rest. Back on the scale, whisk in the yolk, sugar and salt. Add the flour, scatter the yeast over it, and mix with a spatula or hand until no dry flour remains. Cover and rest 15 min in the bowl. The dough is sticky, and this short hydration rest makes it much easier to knead.
  4. Knead. Tip onto an unfloured bench. Stretch-and-fold or slap-and-fold for 6 min. Tear in the butter and knead 3 min more, until it is fully absorbed and the dough is smooth and elastic. Desired dough temperature: 25–26 °C (77–79 °F).
  5. Bulk. Round the dough, return it to the scraped-out bowl, cover, and ferment for 2–2½ h. Warmer dough is faster and cooler dough slower, so go by volume rather than the clock.
  6. Divide & shape. Divide into 4 pieces (~140 g each) and pre-shape loosely. Cover and bench-rest 20 min. Shape into tight rounds and set them well apart on a parchment-lined sheet.
  7. Final proof. Proof covered for about 2 h, until puffy and a floured fingertip leaves a dent that springs back slowly. During the last hour, preheat to 160 °C fan (320 °F convection).
  8. Glaze & bake. Brush with egg white, sprinkle with sesame, and press the seeds on with your palm. Bake ~25 min, turning the tray halfway through, until golden all over. Check at 18 min. Internal temperature ~90 °C (195 °F). Cool on a rack.
What changed, and what did not: The formula is unchanged, including the total flour, water and milk. Only the scald method is different. The original yudane pours 50 g boiling water over 50 g flour and chills the stiff paste in the fridge until fully cold. Here, the same flour is cooked in a looser mix in the microwave. That mix gelatinizes reliably, stays soft, mixes in easily, and needs only a short rest or an ice bath rather than hours in the fridge.
Temperature control: The 250 g scald carries about twice the heat capacity of the 250 g flour. Aim for a scald temperature of about (3 × 25.5 °C − flour temperature) ÷ 2: about 27 °C for flour at 21 °C, cooler in a warm kitchen. Hand kneading adds about 1–2 °C.
Yeast & substitutions: Active dry: whisk it into the cooled scald and wait 5 min before adding the flour. Fresh: crumble it into the scald. In Europe, bread flour is Type 550 (DE), T65 (FR) or “strong” flour (UK). The dough also bakes into a soft sandwich loaf in a 1 lb (8½ × 4½ in) tin; bake until it is about 93 °C inside. To scale up, multiply everything. A larger scald takes longer, so go by the 65 °C reading, not the clock.
