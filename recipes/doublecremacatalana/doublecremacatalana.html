Crema Catalana

With a Hidden Caramel Pool (Double Caramel)

Ingredients

Base Caramel

  • 50 g granulated sugar
  • 15 ml water

Infused Milk

  • 560 ml whole milk (or 2% milk with a splash of heavy cream)
  • 1 small cinnamon stick (or ½ of a large stick)
  • 3 strips lemon zest, pith removed (crystallized citrus substitute is acceptable)
  • 2 strips orange zest, pith removed

Yolk Base

  • 60 g granulated sugar
  • 25 g cornstarch
  • 65 ml whole milk (cold)
  • 5 large egg yolks

Caramel Crust

  • 35 g granulated sugar (or as needed)

Method

  1. Prepare the base caramel: In a small saucepan, combine 50 g of sugar and 15 ml of water. Cook over medium-high heat without stirring until it turns a deep amber. Immediately pour into a 16 cm shallow serving dish. Swirl gently to coat the bottom and set aside to cool and harden.
  2. Steep the milk: In a medium saucepan, combine the 560 ml of milk, cinnamon stick, and citrus zests. Bring to a gentle simmer. Remove from heat, cover, and let steep for 30 minutes. Note: Citrus acid may cause minor curdling in the milk; this is normal.
  3. Mix the yolk base: Place a mixing bowl on a tared scale. Measure 60 g of sugar and 25 g of cornstarch directly into the bowl. Whisk briefly to remove lumps. Add the 65 ml of cold milk and the 5 egg yolks. Using the same whisk, beat vigorously until perfectly smooth.
  4. Strain and temper: Pass the steeped milk through a fine-mesh strainer into a clean pot to remove the aromatics and catch any curdled milk proteins. Bring to a bare simmer. To prevent the yolks from scrambling, temper the mixture by slowly pouring about 100 ml of the hot milk into the cooler yolk base while whisking constantly. Once equalized, pour this stabilized blend back into the saucepan with the remaining hot milk.
  5. Thicken and denature: Cook over medium-low heat, whisking continuously. As it reaches 82°C (180°F), the mixture will thicken significantly and bubble sluggishly. Continue to cook and whisk for exactly 1 minute. This step is critical to denature the alpha-amylase enzyme in the yolks, which would otherwise break down the starch matrix and turn the custard watery during resting.
  6. Pour and chill: Pour the hot custard directly over the hardened caramel base in the serving dish. Let sit at room temperature for 30 minutes, then transfer to the refrigerator uncovered for 12 to 24 hours. The ambient water in the custard will dissolve the hard caramel base into a liquid syrup.
  7. Caramelize and serve: Just before serving, sprinkle an even layer of sugar over the surface. Melt the sugar with a torch until it forms a dark, hard glass crust. Serve immediately.
Yield: Serves 5. Fills exactly one 16 cm (6-inch) shallow dish.
